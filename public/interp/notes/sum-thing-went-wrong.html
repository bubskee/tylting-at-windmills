All grades, rules and ungraded readouts

Two people can get the same score on an arithmetic test and miss entirely different sums. The same goes for silencing a set of neurons and silencing features learned to approximate them. Looking at which digit changes gives us something a single accuracy number throws away.

Original comparisonShabalin & Piotrowski · Addition ablations, Appendix KZoo experiment · saved rows

Published experiment. In Llama 3.1 8B, ablating 28 layer-18 addition neurons cut arithmetic accuracy from 94.3% to 23.6%. Independently removing 87 selected transcoder features cut it to 30.6%. Those two numbers leave an entire error grid unseen. Neurons versus transcoders, Appendix K ↗

Proposed range test. Reveal the neuron-ablation grid. Bet on which sums also break under feature ablation and which wrong answer wins. Compare operand swaps and equal sums with different carry requirements. Our Qwen experiments offer a smaller-scale comparison: a matched neuron and feature can produce very similar directions of output change even when both effects are tiny. Proposed readout. pick a neuron outside the published 28 and look for firing stripes that line up with the errors.
