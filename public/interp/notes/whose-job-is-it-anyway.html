Whose Job Is It Anyway?

Find the shortcut

What does every other tool say?

In a training set where every nurse is a woman and every professor is a man, a pronoun is an awfully convenient clue. Give the classifier a male nurse or a female professor and the shortcut costs it. The paper asks whether tracing the clues a classifier uses can help repair the decisions it gets wrong.

Original studyMarks et al. · Sparse Feature CircuitsZoo experiment · saved rows

Published experiment. Marks et al. trained a nurse-versus-professor classifier on biographies where gender perfectly predicted the training label. Removing sparse features judged irrelevant to occupation raised Pythia-70M’s worst-group profession accuracy from 24.4% to 76.0% on a balanced evaluation, before retraining. Pick which to remove, bet on two biographies with identical career evidence and different pronouns, then reveal accuracy for all four profession-by-gender groups.
